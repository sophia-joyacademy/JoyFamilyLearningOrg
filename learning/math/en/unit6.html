Working with Polynomials

Polynomials are the building blocks of algebra, like the revenue, cost and profit formulas at a plant shop. Add, subtract, multiply and divide them, then run the process backwards by factoring.

📖 Key terms

Monomial · binomial · trinomial

A polynomial with 1, 2 or 3 terms, such as 5x^2, x+3, x^2-2x-15.

Degree

The highest power of the variable. 5x^2-8x+4 has degree 2.

Like terms

Same variables with the same powers. Add or subtract only their coefficients.

Special products

(a\pm b)^2=a^2\pm2ab+b^2 and (a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2.

Remainder & Factor Theorems

Dividing f(x) by x-c leaves remainder f(c). If f(c)=0, then x-c is a factor.

GCF · prime polynomial

Greatest common factor: the biggest thing every term shares. A prime polynomial cannot be factored further.

⭐ Summary: perfect squares & difference of squares

These two patterns show up everywhere. Learn to spot them and you can expand and factor in one step, with no FOIL.

Binomial square

(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2

(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2

Expanding gives a perfect square trinomial. Remember the middle term 2ab: (a+b)^2\ne a^2+b^2.

(5x-3)^2=25x^2-30x+9

Difference of squares

(a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2

The two middle terms cancel, so only two terms remain. Both must be squares with a minus between.

(11z+7)(11z-7)=121z^2-49

A sum of squares such as x^2+25 is prime.

How to recognize them

Perfect square trinomial: first and last terms are squares and the middle is \pm2\sqrt{\text{first}}\sqrt{\text{last}}.

4x^2+12x+9=(2x+3)^2

Difference of squares: two terms, subtract, both squares.

25x^2-49=(5x-7)(5x+7)

➕ Add, subtract & evaluate

Set two quadratics f and g. Subtracting means distributing a negative sign. Try the textbook pair: f = 3x²−5x+7 and g = x²−4x−3.

✖️ FOIL method

Multiply two binomials (ax+b)(cx+d): First, Outer, Inner, Last, then combine. Press Next step to reveal one step at a time, Back to undo, Reset to start over the like middle terms. Try the textbook product (x−7)(2x+4) = 2x²−10x−28.

🟦 Area-model multiplier

Each partial product is one rectangle of the big rectangle. Add the four pieces to get (ax+b)(cx+d). Try 17×13 as (10+7)(10+3) = 221 by choosing x = 10.

🔍 Special products: compare the patterns

Set A=ax and B=b. See all three special products side by side and notice which ones have a middle term. Pick a pattern to see its steps.

Mental math: 51² = (50+1)² = 2601 and 49·51 = (50−1)(50+1) = 2499.

➗ Synthetic division stepper

Divide a cubic by x-c. Bring down, multiply by c, add, repeat. The last number is the remainder, and it equals f(c). Try the defaults: 2x³+3x²+x+8 ÷ (x+2) leaves remainder 2.

🧲 GCF simulator

Set three terms kx^p. The GCF is the biggest number that divides every coefficient times the lowest power of x. Pull it out, divide each term, then check by distributing.

🧪 Factoring ax^2+bx+c: trial & error vs the ac method

Two ways to factor the same trinomial. Trial and error: try factor pairs of a and c until the middle term equals b. ac method: find two numbers with product ac and sum b, split the middle term, then group.

🪞 Factoring special products

Pick a pattern, then check each clue: is the first term a square, is the last term a square, and does the middle term equal twice the product of the roots?

🧩 Factoring lab

Build k(ax^2+bx+c). The lab expands it, pulls out the GCF, then factors what is left. Spot perfect squares and differences of squares, or a prime trinomial.
