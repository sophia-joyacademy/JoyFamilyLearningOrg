📖 Key terms

Quadratic equation

ax^2+bx+c=0 with a\ne0. Its solutions are the zeros of y=ax^2+bx+c.

Square root property

If x^2=k then x=\pm\sqrt{k} (for k\ge0). Isolate the squared part first.

Zero product property

If ab=0 then a=0 or b=0. Only works when the product is zero.

Double root

A repeated factor like (x-3)^2=0 gives one solution. The parabola just touches the x-axis.

Factoring

x^2+bx+c=(x+m)(x+n) where m+n=b and mn=c.

Intercept form

f(x)=a(x-p)(x-q) has zeros p,q. One more point finds a.

🎯 How many solutions?

Solve ax^2+bx+c=k by graphing y=ax^2+bx+c and the line y=k. The crossings are the solutions: 2, 1 (a double root) or 0. Try the potato: a=-16 does not fit here, so try x^2-8x+15=0 for two, then slide k up to the vertex for one.

🧠 Solve by reasoning

Two ways to solve without graphing. Square root property: isolate (x-h)^2, then take \pm\sqrt{}. Zero product property: if the factors multiply to 0, one of them is 0.

🧩 Factoring trinomials

To factor ax^2+bx+c find two numbers with product ac and sum b. Signs tell you: positive c means same signs; negative c means opposite signs. With b = 0 and c a negative square you get a difference of squares. Then solve with the zero product property.

✍️ Write an equation from zeros

Zeros p and q give f(x)=a(x-p)(x-q). A point (x_0,y_0) on the curve fixes a. Try the textbook example: zeros −5 and 4 through (−4, −16), or the rocket: zeros 1 and 6 through (5, 8).

🚧 Common traps

Don't divide by x

x^2-10x=0. Dividing by x loses the solution x=0. Factor: x(x-10)=0 gives 0 and 10.

Product must be 0

(x-2)(x+3)=7 does not mean a factor is 7. Move everything to one side first.

Negative square

x^2=-16 has no real solution; no real number squares to a negative.

Not a perfect square

(x+5)^2\ne x^2+25. It equals x^2+10x+25. And x^2+25 does not factor over the reals.
