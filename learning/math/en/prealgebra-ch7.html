Properties of Real Numbers

Sort numbers into rational and irrational, learn the rules that let you rearrange and regroup, expand with the distributive property, use identities and inverses, and convert between units of measurement.

Key terms

Rational number

A number that can be written as p/q with p, q integers and q ≠ 0. Its decimal stops or repeats.

Irrational number

A number that cannot be written as a ratio of two integers. Its decimal never stops and never repeats.

Real number

A number that is either rational or irrational: every point on the number line.

Additive identity

0. Adding 0 to any number does not change it.

Multiplicative identity

1. Multiplying any number by 1 does not change it.

Additive inverse

The opposite of a number. The additive inverse of a is −a.

Multiplicative inverse

The reciprocal of a number. The multiplicative inverse of a is 1/a (a ≠ 0).

🔀 Commutative and associative properties

Commutative: change the order. Associative: change the grouping. They work for addition and multiplication. Try subtraction and division to see them fail.

Why do we care?

You can reorder and regroup to make mental math easy:

  • 17 + 8 + 3 = 17 + 3 + 8 = 20 + 8 = 28
  • 4 · 7 · 25 = 4 · 25 · 7 = 100 · 7 = 700

Careful: these rules only apply to + and ×. Subtraction and division need the order and grouping as written.

        Strategies cheat sheet

        Rational or irrational?
        1. Can it be written as a fraction of integers? Then it is rational.
        2. Decimal stops or repeats: rational.
        3. Decimal never ends and never repeats (π, √2, √50): irrational.
        4. √ of a perfect square is a whole number, so it is rational.
        Which property?
        1. Same numbers, different order: commutative.
        2. Same order, different grouping: associative.
        3. Outside number multiplied into parentheses: distributive.
        4. Adding 0 or multiplying by 1: identity.
        Distribute
        1. Multiply the outside number by the first term inside.
        2. Multiply it by the second term.
        3. Keep the sign between them (+ or −).
        4. A negative outside flips every sign inside.
        Zero rules
        1. a · 0 = 0.
        2. 0 ÷ a = 0 when a ≠ 0.
        3. a ÷ 0 is undefined. Never allowed.
        Convert units
        1. Write the starting amount with its unit.
        2. Build a unit fraction: wanted unit on top, old unit on the bottom.
        3. Multiply. The old units cancel.
        4. Check the size makes sense: a smaller unit gives a bigger number.
        Temperature
        1. °C → °F: multiply by 9/5, then add 32.
        2. °F → °C: subtract 32, then multiply by 5/9.
        3. °C → K: add 273.15.
