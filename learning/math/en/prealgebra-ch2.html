The Language of Algebra

Variables, expressions and equations, plus the tools for working with them: order of operations, like terms, balanced equations, factors and multiples.

Key terms

Expression vs equation

Expression: numbers, variables and operations, with no equal sign. Example: 3x + 5.

Equation: two expressions joined by =. Example: 3x + 5 = 20.

Evaluate: replace the variable by a number and find the value.

Parts of an expression

In 4x² + 7x + 9 the terms are 4x², 7x and 9.

Coefficient: the number multiplying the variable (4 and 7). A constant has no variable (9).

Like terms: constants, or terms with the same variable and exponent.

Solutions

A solution of an equation is a value that makes it true when substituted.

Finding it is solving the equation.

Factors & multiples

If a · b = m, then a and b are factors of m and m is a multiple of each. “m is divisible by n” means m is a multiple of n.

Prime & composite

Prime: counting number greater than 1 whose only factors are 1 and itself.

Composite: counting number that is not prime (and greater than 1).

Prime factorization & LCM

Prime factorization: a number written as a product of primes.

LCM: the smallest number that is a multiple of both numbers.

Exponents
aⁿ = a · a · … · a (n factors)

Read “a to the nth power”. Examples: 2³ = 2 · 2 · 2 = 8, and 5² = 25 (“5 squared”).

🧮 Order of operations & evaluate lab

Type an expression with ( ), ^ for exponents, + − × ÷, and optionally the variable x. Slide x to evaluate. The lab solves it in the correct order, one step at a time.

    Order of operations (PEMDAS)
    1. Parentheses and other grouping symbols, innermost first.
    2. Exponents.
    3. Multiplication and Division, left to right (equal priority).
    4. Addition and Subtraction, left to right (equal priority).
    Watch out
    • Multiplying does not always come before dividing: go left to right.
    • 4² means 4 · 4 = 16, not 4 · 2.
    • 3 + 2 × 4 = 11, not 20.

      🌳 Prime factor tree

      Split a number into any two factors, then keep splitting until every branch ends in a prime.

      1. Write the number at the top.
      2. Split it into two factors (any pair works!).
      3. Circle a factor when it is prime. It stops.
      4. Keep splitting the others until all branches end in primes.
      5. The circled primes, multiplied together, make the number.

      Try different first splits for the same number. The tree changes shape but the primes at the bottom are always the same.

      🔁 Least common multiple lab

      Two numbers from 1 to 200. Compare their lists of multiples, then use the prime factors method.

      Multiples

      Prime factors method

      1. Find the prime factorization of each number.
      2. Line up matching primes in columns.
      3. Bring down each column.
      4. Multiply to get the LCM.

      🪜 Ladder (short division): GCF and LCM of two numbers

      Divide both numbers by a common prime again and again, until the bottom numbers share no factor except 1.

      1. Write the two numbers side by side. Divide both by a common prime on the left.
      2. Write the quotients underneath. Repeat.
      3. Stop when the bottom numbers have no common factor except 1.
      4. GCF = multiply the purple divisors on the left.
      5. LCM = multiply the purple divisors and the red bottom numbers.

      🪜 Ladder for 3 or 4 numbers

      Do the GCF first with divisors that fit every number. Then keep going with divisors that fit only some of them.

      1. Write the numbers side by side.
      2. GCF first: divide by a prime that fits every number (purple). Repeat until none does.
      3. GCF = multiply the purple divisors.
      4. Then LCM: divide by a prime that fits two or more numbers (teal). Numbers it doesn’t fit come straight down.
      5. Stop when no prime fits two numbers. The bottom numbers are red.
      6. LCM = multiply purple, teal and red.

      Strategies cheat sheet

      Is it a solution?
      1. Substitute the number for the variable.
      2. Simplify both sides.
      3. True statement? It is a solution. False? It is not.
      Solve with + or −
      1. Use the Addition or Subtraction Property to isolate the variable (undo the operation).
      2. Simplify both sides.
      3. Check the solution.
      Find all factors
      1. Divide by 1, 2, 3 … until the quotient is smaller than the divisor.
      2. A whole-number quotient gives a factor pair.
      3. List all factors from smallest to largest.
      Is it prime?
      1. Test primes in order (2, 3, 5, 7, …).
      2. Stop when you find a factor or when the quotient is smaller than the divisor.
      3. A prime factor found → composite. None → prime.
      Prime factorization
      • Tree: split into any factor pair; keep splitting until every branch is prime; multiply the primes.
      • Ladder: divide by 2 until it no longer fits, then 3, then 5 … until the quotient is prime.
      Find the LCM
      • Listing: list multiples of each; the smallest in both lists.
      • Prime factors: factor each; bring down each column of primes; multiply.
