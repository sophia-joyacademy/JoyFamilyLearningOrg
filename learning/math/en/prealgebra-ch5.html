Decimals

Fractions with a power of 10 underneath. Read, compare and round decimals; add, subtract, multiply and divide them; convert to fractions; solve equations; find averages, ratios, unit prices and square roots.

Key terms

Equivalent decimals

Decimals that name the same number, such as 0.5 = 0.50 = 0.500. Adding zeros at the end does not change the value.

Repeating decimal

The last digit or group of digits repeats forever. 1/3 = 0.3 and 1/11 = 0.09.

Mean

The average: the sum of the values divided by how many values there are.

Median

The middle value when the data is in order. Half the values are at or below it, half at or above.

Mode

The value that appears most often.

Ratio

Compares two numbers with the same unit. a to b, a:b, or ab.

Rate & unit rate

A rate compares different units (miles per hour). A unit rate has 1 in the denominator. A unit price is the price of 1 item.

Circle

Radius r: center to the circle. Diameter d = 2r. Circumference: the distance around.

Square root

If m = n² and n ≥ 0, then \sqrt{m} = n. \sqrt{25} = 5 because 5² = 25.

🔢 Place value, names, fractions and rounding

Type any decimal (up to 6 places). See its place-value chart, how to read it, its fraction, and round it to a place you pick.

Name a decimal
  1. Say the whole-number part.
  2. Say “and” for the decimal point.
  3. Say the digits after the point as a whole number.
  4. Finish with the place name of the last digit.
Round a decimal
  1. Find the place you are rounding to.
  2. Look at the digit right after it.
  3. 5 or more: add 1 to your digit. Less than 5: leave it.
  4. Drop the digits after the place.

⚖️ Compare decimals

Line up the decimal points, add zeros so both numbers have the same length, then compare from the left.

vs
Watch out

More digits does not mean bigger. 0.45 has 2 places and 0.5 has 1, but 0.5 = 0.50 is bigger.

Orange zeros are placeholders. They do not change the value.

➕ Add & subtract decimals

Line up the decimal points, fill in zeros, add or subtract like whole numbers, and bring the point straight down.

+
    Rules
    • Point under point. Zeros are placeholders.
    • The decimal point in the answer is in line with the points above.
    • If you subtract a bigger number from a smaller one, the answer is negative.

    ✖️ Multiply decimals

    Multiply as whole numbers, then count the decimal places in both factors to place the point.

    ×
      Shortcut: powers of 10

      Multiply: move the point right. Divide: move it left. Add zeros when you run out of digits.

      ➗ Divide decimals

      Make the divisor a whole number by moving both decimal points the same number of places, then divide.

      ÷
        Rules
        • Move the point in the divisor all the way right. Move the dividend’s point the same number of places.
        • The decimal point in the quotient sits right above the point in the dividend.
        • Same signs: positive. Different signs: negative.
        • A bar over digits means they repeat forever.

          Strategies cheat sheet

          Decimal → fraction
          1. Whole-number part: zero means a proper fraction, otherwise a mixed number.
          2. Numerator: the digits after the point.
          3. Denominator: the place value of the last digit.
          4. Simplify.
          Add or subtract
          1. Line up the decimal points.
          2. Fill in zeros as placeholders.
          3. Add or subtract as whole numbers.
          4. Put the point straight down.
          Multiply
          1. Find the sign.
          2. Multiply as whole numbers, ignoring the points.
          3. Product decimal places = places in factor 1 + places in factor 2.
          4. Use zeros as placeholders if needed.
          Divide
          1. Find the sign.
          2. Move the divisor’s point to the right end; move the dividend’s point the same number of places.
          3. Divide. Keep the point above the point.
          Mean, median, mode
          1. Mean: sum ÷ count. Check it is between the least and greatest values.
          2. Median: order the list. Odd count: the middle. Even count: mean of the two middle values.
          3. Mode: the most frequent value.
          Best buy
          1. Write price ÷ amount for each item.
          2. Divide to get the unit price.
          3. The lower unit price is the better buy.
