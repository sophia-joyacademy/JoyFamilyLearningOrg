📖 Key terms

Place value

A digit's value depends on its place. In 4,305,210 the 3 is worth 300,000.

Period

Groups of three digits: ones, thousands, millions. Commas separate periods.

Power of ten

10² = 100, 10³ = 1,000. Multiplying by 10ⁿ adds n zeros to the end.

Estimate

Round first, then calculate, to check that an answer is reasonable.

Order of operations

Parentheses, then × and ÷ left to right, then + and − left to right.

Bar model

A picture of a word problem: bars show parts, totals and differences.

🎯 Rounding

Pick a place, then type a number (up to 999,999) or use ▲ ▼ on the digits. The target digit is purple; the digit to its right (the decider) is orange.

  1. Find the given place.
  2. Look at the digit to its right.
  3. 5 or more → add 1 to the place digit (9 becomes 0 and carries left). Less than 5 → leave it.
  4. Replace everything to the right with zeros.

⚡ Powers of ten

Multiplying by 10, 100, 1,000 adds zeros to the end; dividing by them takes the same number of zeros off. The exponent tells how many zeros. (Dividing here always starts from a number that ends in enough zeros, so the answer stays whole.)

🚧 Common traps

Left to right

24 ÷ 6 × 2 = 8, not 2. × and ÷ share a level; go left to right. Same for + and −.

Zero as placeholder

905,004 is nine hundred five thousand, four. Keep the zeros in their places.

Zeros when multiplying by 10ⁿ

35 × 100 = 3,500: add two zeros, not one. And 3,500 ÷ 100 = 35: take two zeros off.

Check the remainder

The remainder must be less than the divisor. Check: quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend.
