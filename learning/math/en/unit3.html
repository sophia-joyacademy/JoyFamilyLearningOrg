Two-Variable Statistics

Turn a cloud of dots into a story. Draw a line of fit, read its slope and intercept, measure the fit with residuals and r, and know when “goes together” does not mean “causes”.

🗺️ Concept map: two-variable statistics

The big picture of the unit (Lessons 3.1 – 3.6). Click any box to see what it means, with an example.

Key terms

Bivariate data

Pairs of numbers (x, y) that describe two variables for the same person or thing.

Scatter plot

A graph with one dot per pair. Its pattern shows the direction (up or down), strength and form (line or curve).

Linear model

A line ŷ = mx + b that describes the trend. The hat on ŷ means predicted y.

Slope and y-intercept

Slope: the change in predicted y for each 1 more x. Intercept: the predicted y when x = 0.

Interpolate / extrapolate

Predict inside the data’s x-range (usually reliable) or outside it (risky).

Residual

Actual y minus predicted ŷ. Positive means the dot is above the line, negative means below.

Residual plot

A plot of x against residuals. Random scatter around 0 means a good linear fit; a curve means it is not.

Correlation coefficient r

A number from −1 to 1. Its sign gives the direction and its size gives the strength of the linear relationship.

Causal relationship

A change in x produces a change in y. Correlation alone does not prove it.

        🔍 Correlation vs causation

        For each pair, decide: does x cause y, or is something else going on? (Lesson 3.6)

        Checklist before saying “causes”

        • Is there a sensible way x could change y?
        • Could a third factor drive both?
        • Could it be a coincidence (a small or odd data set)?
        • Strong evidence usually comes from a controlled experiment, not just a scatter plot.

        Strategies cheat sheet

        Describe a scatter plot
        • Direction: up (positive) or down (negative).
        • Strength: tight or loose.
        • Form: line or curve.
        • Mention outliers.
        Interpret a model ŷ = mx + b
        • “For each 1 more x, the model predicts y to change by m.”
        • “When x is 0, the model predicts b.” Check that x = 0 makes sense.
        Judge the fit
        • Residual = y − ŷ.
        • Residual plot: random scatter = good, curve = bad.
        • |r| near 1 = strong linear, near 0 = weak linear.
        Predict wisely
        • Inside the x-range: interpolation.
        • Outside: extrapolation, be careful.
        • Correlation ≠ causation.
