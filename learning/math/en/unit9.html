📖 Key terms

Perfect square

(x+b)^2=x^2+2bx+b^2. The constant is half the middle coefficient, squared.

Completing the square

Add (b/2)^2 to x^2+bx to make (x+b/2)^2. Add it to both sides.

Quadratic formula

x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a} solves ax^2+bx+c=0.

Discriminant

b^2-4ac: positive gives 2 solutions, zero gives 1, negative gives none.

Vertex form

a(x-h)^2+k has vertex (h,k). Minimum if a>0, maximum if a<0.

Three forms

Standard ax^2+bx+c, factored a(x-r)(x-s), vertex a(x-h)^2+k.

🟦 Completing the square

Solve ax^2+bx+c=0 step by step: divide by a, move c, add (b/2a)^2 to both sides, factor, and take \pm\sqrt{}. Try x^2+8x-48=0 (answers 4 and −12) or 16x^2+40x-11=0.

🧮 The quadratic formula

Read a, b, c from ax^2+bx+c=0 (watch the signs!), then substitute. The discriminant tells how many solutions to expect.

🎯 Vertex form and the best price

Complete the square on ax^2+bx+c to get a(x-h)^2+k. Then (h,k) is the highest or lowest point. Try -x^2-14x-60: a maximum of −11 at x=-7.

🚧 Common traps

Add to both sides

x^2+14x=-24: add 49 (half of 14, squared) to both sides, not 28. Solutions −2 and −12.

Keep both roots

(x-3)^2=16 gives x-3=\pm4, so x=7 and x=-1.

Sign of b and c

For x^2+3x=10 move first: c=-10. For -b with b=-8 you get +8, and (-8)^2=64.

Whole numerator

In \dfrac{8\pm\sqrt{40}}{4} divide every term: \dfrac{4\pm\sqrt{10}}{2}, not 2\pm\sqrt{10}.

a ≠ 1 in vertex form

Factor a out of the x terms first: 2x^2+8x+4=2(x+2)^2-4. Remember to multiply the added number by a.

Not a perfect square

(x-5)^2\ne x^2+25. It is x^2-10x+25.
